										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<p>You&#8217;ve seen the headlines, or TikToks — the <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/discover/traffic-cones-on-statues">traffic cones on statues</a>, the <a href="https://www.nbcboston.com/news/local/are-scottish-soccer-fans-drinking-all-of-the-beer-in-boston/3966171/">pubs drunk dry</a>, the <a href="https://www.tiktok.com/tag/tartanarmy">throngs of men in kilts</a>.</p>
<p>The Tartan Army took Boston by storm in the best possible way last week during Scotland&#8217;s run of games in Foxborough, and The Boston Globe has <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/15/sports/scotland-tartan-army-fenway-park/">chronicled</a> the <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/19/lifestyle/world-cup-scots-boston-beer/">happy</a> <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/17/sports/traffic-cones-boston-statues-scottish-fans-world-cup/">chaos</a> <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/20/sports/world-cup-scotland-fans-boston/">with</a> <a href="https://www.instagram.com/p/DZyL2wNRs4U/">zeal</a>. &#8220;As they swept through the city with giddy abandon, all the logistical concerns fell aside, forgotten in a swirl of song and drink and shared devotion,&#8221; assistant news editor Peter Schworm <a href="https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/06/22/metro/scottish-world-cup-fans-in-boston/">wrote</a>. &#8220;Boston quickly forgot itself, shedding its skeptical reserve and embracing the joy of the event in full measure, cast under the Scottish spell.&#8221;</p>
<p>On Saturday, the newspaper went so far as to publish a full-page ad thanking Scottish fans for &#8220;the joy you brought to our city.&#8221;</p>

<p>Other community institutions have expressed their gratitude publicly, too: After the Tartan Army&#8217;s <a href="https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-9c6AygH0ZU">viral musical takeover of Fenway Park</a>, The Boston Red Sox sent a <a href="https://x.com/ScotlandNT/status/2068620187054547445/photo/1">letter</a> to the Scottish FA thanking and celebrating the team&#8217;s fans for showing &#8220;a spirit that has no equivalent in American sport.&#8221;</p>
<p>&#8220;They treated our home like their own,&#8221; Red Sox President Sam Kennedy wrote, &#8220;and we are better for it.&#8221;</p>

										<content:encoded><![CDATA[<p>When The Washington Post&#8217;s management axed Book World in February, it wasn&#8217;t just one casualty among hundreds of layoffs; it was the latest high-profile death blow to a newspaper book review section in more than two decades of a thousand cuts.</p>
<p>The AP <a href="https://dankennedy.net/2025/08/08/the-associated-press-tells-its-book-critics-that-its-ending-weekly-reviews/">stopped publishing book reviews last fall</a>. Metro dailies <a href="https://www.salon.com/2001/07/19/book_reviews/">like the San Francisco Chronicle</a> whittled down or eliminated standalone book sections a quarter century ago, leaving The New York Times Book Review &#8220;the last discrete newspaper books section standing,&#8221; former Washington Post nonfiction book critic Becca Rothfeld <a href="https://www.newyorker.com/books/page-turner/the-death-of-book-world">wrote in The New Yorker</a>. (After the Post layoffs, The New Yorker hired her as a staff writer.)</p>
<p>Bookseller <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/joshthelibromancer.bsky.social">Josh Cook</a> has taken note of the black hole swallowing newspaper-published book reviews. In the past, &#8220;you could just encounter [book reviews] in your world&#8221; via the physical presence of local newspapers, he told me in the back office of <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/">Porter Square Books</a> in Cambridge, Mass., on a recent Friday afternoon. (He pointed to now-shuttered alt weeklies <a href="https://thephoenix.com/">The Boston Phoenix</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigBoston">DigBoston</a> as another extinct source of book reviews.)</p>
<p>So Cook, a co-owner of PSB, decided to do something to bring newspaper-style book reviews back. The <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/porter-square-review-books">Porter Square Review of Books</a> launched this month. The store&#8217;s booksellers and writers-in-residence have begun publishing weekly(ish) book reviews on its website, on Thursdays; at about 500 words, these are deeper looks at books than the couple of sentences you&#8217;ll find describing &#8220;staff picks&#8221; in-store (or on your Goodreads or StoryGraph). The team shares them on <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/portersqbooks.bsky.social">Bluesky</a> and will round them up in a <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/form/sign-up-for-porter-square-books">monthly newsletter</a>.</p>
<p>&#8220;A lot of bookselling is the first couple steps of writing a book review anyway,&#8221; Cook told me. &#8220;You assess it to see if you like it, you figure out how you&#8217;re going to describe it to other people, you decide whether you&#8217;re going to finish it&#8230;We already are part of the way there. Why not just see if we could do it?&#8221;</p>
<blockquote class="bluesky-embed" data-bluesky-uri="at://did:plc:d2hirmilhg3jwatp2qvtligc/app.bsky.feed.post/3mnifecz3pc2n" data-bluesky-cid="bafyreiev7a3xetryokarj5j5s6o765uc5xrprkgng6qjjdvifisxnertzq" data-bluesky-embed-color-mode="system">

<p>We&#8217;ve covered some examples of indie bookstores and local media <a href="https://www.niemanlab.org/2026/02/the-tampa-bay-times-starts-a-monthly-book-club-for-news-stories/">joining</a> <a href="https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/07/missouris-oldest-black-bookstore-just-closed-the-kansas-city-defender-plans-to-reopen-it-as-a-newsroom-and-public-archive/">forces</a>. But watching the broader erosion of local media, Cook has argued that <em>publishers</em> — with their writers and editors, printers and distribution networks and warehouses — have the infrastructure to help rebuild American journalism. Last year, he described a &#8220;swing for the fences&#8221; vision in the third of a series of essays for Literary Hub on what the publishing industry should do in the face of a second Trump administration. &#8220;Imagine major publishers collaborating on a weekly newspaper based on the Lit Hub model,&#8221; he <a href="https://lithub.com/the-reforestation-of-american-civic-life-what-publishing-can-do-in-the-face-of-the-trump/">wrote</a>, &#8220;filled with long form and narrative journalism written by authors with forthcoming or recently released books, distributed to bookstores and libraries to give away for free.&#8221;</p>
<p>Cook <a href="https://bsky.app/profile/joshthelibromancer.bsky.social/post/3me2gdddqbk2g">reupped that essay after the Post layoffs</a>, writing on Bluesky that big publishers &#8220;have the resources to rebuild the media wasteland&#8221; and &#8220;if they want people to discover books at all, it kinda looks like they need to.&#8221;</p>
<p>While publishers have yet to rally to that call to arms, it got Cook thinking about what Porter Square Books could feasibly do in this spirit. Book reviews seemed like the obvious starting point.</p>
<p>A lack of books coverage, including book reviews, &#8220;hurts everyone in the books ecosystem: readers, writers, publishers, and, of course, bookstores,&#8221; Porter Square Books <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/announcing-porter-square-review-books">said</a> in its Book Review announcement. &#8220;Putting that in the context of contemporary attacks on the rewards and pleasures of deep thinking and imaginative creating only makes things more dire. Well, we&#8217;re not just going to complain about it!&#8221;</p>
<p><div class="storybreak-simple"><span></span></div></p>
<p>Cook has written book reviews for sites like <a href="https://themillions.com/">The Millions</a>, <a href="https://therumpus.net/">The Rumpus</a>, and <a href="https://lithub.com/bookslut-is-dead-long-live-bookslut/">Bookslut</a>. The first level of a good book review, he said, is to &#8220;give readers enough to know if they want to read it.&#8221; That doesn&#8217;t just mean a summary; it should give a sense of style and tone. &#8220;After that, I like reviews that put the book in its various contexts,&#8221; he added, whether its genre, that genre&#8217;s history, or &#8220;the broader scope of books.&#8221; Finally, he thinks a good book review should &#8220;give prospective readers some tools to read the book if they decide to — some perspectives, some things to be aware about&#8221; that will likely enhance the reading experience.</p>
<p>Cook <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/blog/review-plastic-prism-void-part-1">wrote</a> the first PSRB review, of Violet Allen&#8217;s <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/blog/review-plastic-prism-void-part-1"><em>Plastic, Prism, Void: Part One</em></a>, himself. He also edits the Review. It will mostly cover new releases, and the store tries to &#8220;throw our support behind smaller presses and less well-known authors, so you&#8217;ll probably see a bit more debuts, a bit more lesser-known books,&#8221; Cook said.</p>

<p>Reviews will span genres and authors — a middle-grade review is in the works now — but what gets reviewed will mostly depend on booksellers&#8217; personal tastes and appetites. Last week, PSB events &amp; marketing assistant Isabel Miranda Kidwell <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/blog/review-women">reviewed Chloe Caldwell&#8217;s 2014 novella <em>Women</em></a>, which she discovered on the store&#8217;s &#8220;damages&#8221; shelf. &#8220;This little book, simple cream-colored and bent at its left corner (more so now, actually, from my grip) is one I will never let off my bookshelf,&#8221; she wrote, &#8220;and this review is my small attempt at getting others who, like me, had previously never heard of it — too bombarded by the &#8216;canon&#8217; classics or today&#8217;s NYT bestsellers — to pick it up and read it.&#8221;</p>
<p>Because many PSB booksellers are horror aficionados, Cook expects that genre to be well represented. Jacob Orlando, a bookseller at the Boston location of Porter Square Books, recently <a href="https://portersquarebooks.com/blog/review-caretaker">reviewed <em>The Caretaker</em></a> by Marcus Kliewer. He called it &#8220;a tense and terrifying trip perfect for a cabin weekend, a long drive or just a few late nights hoping your lights don&#8217;t flicker&#8221; while calling out some sections that &#8220;drag&#8221; and &#8220;loose threads.&#8221;</p>
<p>I was curious whether Cook expects to publish wholly negative reviews. &#8220;There is a place for negative reviews in the world of books, but I&#8217;m not really sure it&#8217;s in the 500-plus word stuff that we are doing,&#8221; he said. For one t
